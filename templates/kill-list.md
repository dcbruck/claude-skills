# Kill list

Copy this file to your project root as `kill-list.md`. This is a starter: the tells most LLM drafts share, grouped by how much damage they do. Add your own as you catch them in your edits. The list only gets sharp when it carries the patterns *you* keep deleting.

`/ghost` and `/fleet` scan every draft against this file, soul-level first, surface last. A single soul-level hit means a rewrite of that section. Surface hits get fixed in place.

## Soul-level (the piece has no point of view)

- **Unanchored confidence.** Punchy claims with no client, number, scene, or anecdote behind them. Reads like a guru.
- **The safe take.** Correct, well-written, and would not cost a single follower. Nothing is at stake.
- **Explainer with no stakes.** Teaches clearly but the writer is nowhere in it.
- **Performed humanity.** Verbal tics and "personal" details deployed on schedule. The pieces are there, the music is missing.
- **Flat temperature.** Every paragraph at the same emotional intensity.

## Voice-level (a real person would not say it this way)

- **Over-formality.** "Per our previous conversation." "Please don't hesitate to reach out." "I wanted to take a moment to."
- **Contraction avoidance.** "It is", "do not", "cannot" where speech would contract. One deliberate uncontracted beat per piece, maximum.
- **The hedge pile.** "I was wondering if perhaps we might consider potentially exploring."
- **Importance puffery.** "Crucial", "essential", "vital", "critical" doing the work a specific consequence should do.
- **Sign-off mismatch.** A warm email closed with "Sincerely" or "Kind regards."

## Structural (the skeleton is machine-shaped)

- **Negate-then-assert.** "X isn't Y, it's Z." In every costume: "not because A but because B", "didn't change X, it changed Y", "stops being X and becomes Y". State the positive directly.
- **The mirrored pair.** Two parallel sentences that demonstrate a contrast. Negate-then-assert wearing a different coat.
- **The trio list.** Three items every time, whether the real count is two or seven. Includes trios merged into one flowing sentence.
- **Fragment stacking.** "The prompts. The workflows. The systems." Percussion used for enumeration.
- **Concluding summary.** A final paragraph that rephrases the piece without adding anything.
- **Post-insight explanation.** An insight, then the next sentence explains it. "Which means..." "In other words..."
- **Analogy overexplain.** Mapping every element of the metaphor back to the literal subject.
- **Label-colon reveal.** "The problem: nobody reads it." "The fix: shorter." Stock cadence.
- **Restatement staircase.** Three sentences that each restate the previous one at slightly higher altitude.
- **Command cascade.** "Ship it. Measure it. Fix it. Repeat." Imperatives in a row as a closer.
- **Sweeping-history opener.** "For decades, marketers have..." "Since the dawn of..."
- **Aphoristic-maxim closer.** The portable life-lesson kicker. Land on a concrete instead.
- **Predictable paragraph rhythm.** Long, short, long, short, on a metronome.
- **Bullet flood.** Bullets used for arguments instead of inventory.

## Surface (word-level tells)

- **Banned words.** delve, landscape, supercharge, game-changer, next-level, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, tapestry, paradigm, synergy, unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, harness, navigate, streamline. Add your own.
- **Banned phrases.** "In today's fast-paced world." "Let's dive in." "It's worth noting." "At the end of the day." "Take it to the next level."
- **Stock transitions.** Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, That being said, Building on this, With that in mind.
- **Em dashes as a tic.** One per piece at most, or none. Commas, periods, and colons do the job.
- **Superficial `-ing` analysis.** "Highlighting the importance of..." "Underscoring the need for..." A participle pretending to be a point.
- **Vague quantity.** "Many years" (say 13). "Significant results" (say the number). "Several clients" (name the count).
